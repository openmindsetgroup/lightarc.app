Why the trend tells you more than today's number

You step on the scale, and the number is up. You did nothing different. Tomorrow it's down again. If that sounds familiar, the number isn't broken: a single weigh-in moves around from day to day with things like water, salt and meals.

A trend line looks past those swings. It shows the direction you're actually heading, so one high or low morning doesn't decide how your week feels.

What a trend line does

A trend line is a moving average of your weigh-ins. Each new weigh-in nudges the line a little instead of jerking it around. A surprising number on one day barely moves it. A change that keeps showing up, day after day, moves it steadily.

That's why two people looking at the same week can read it so differently. Day by day, it looks like a zigzag. As a trend, it might be a gentle, steady line.

How Lightarc works out your trend

  • Daily average first. If you weigh in more than once a day, those weigh-ins are averaged together for that day.
  • A moving average that handles gaps. Missed a few days? The trend accounts for the time between weigh-ins rather than treating them as back to back.
  • You choose how smooth. In Settings you can pick Gentle (about 20 days), Standard (about 10 days, recommended for most people) or Responsive (about 5 days).
  • Weekly rate. Lightarc reads the slope of your trend over the last 14 days (or 7, if that's all there is) and shows it as a weekly rate.

Reading the chart

  • Look at the trend line's direction, not at where today's weigh-in landed.
  • The weekly rate tells you how fast the trend is moving. Change since start and distance to your optional goal are on the same screen.
  • If you track a GLP-1 or other medication, dose days, dose changes and on-medication periods can be marked on the same chart, so you can see your log in one place.

What a trend can't tell you

A trend line describes your own log. It isn't medical advice, and it doesn't explain why your weight changed. For questions about your health or your medication, talk to your doctor or prescriber.
