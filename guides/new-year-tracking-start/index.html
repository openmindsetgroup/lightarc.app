Starting to track your weight this year, without the daily stress

A new year is a natural time to start keeping track. The hard part usually isn't stepping on the scale. It's what the number does to your mood the next morning, when it's up for no reason you can see.

This guide is about setting up a way of tracking that you can live with past January.

Expect the number to bounce

Your weight on any single morning moves around with things like water, salt and meals. A jump of a pound or two overnight is the scale doing its job, not a verdict on your week.

So before you start, decide what you'll actually look at. If it's today's number, every up day will feel like a setback. If it's the direction over time, one up day barely matters.

Look at the trend, not the day

A trend line is a moving average of your weigh-ins. Each new weigh-in nudges it a little. A one-off high or low barely moves it; a change that keeps showing up moves it steadily. (There's more on this in Why the trend tells you more than today's number.)

Pick a rhythm you'll keep

There's no single right schedule. Some people weigh in every morning; some prefer once a week. What matters for a trend is that you keep going.

  • Daily or weekly reminder. Lightarc can remind you once a day or once a week, and it skips the reminder if you've already logged that day.
  • Several weigh-ins in one day are fine. They're averaged together for that day's trend.
  • Missed a few days? The trend accounts for the gap rather than treating your weigh-ins as back to back.
  • How quickly the line reacts is up to you. In Settings you can pick Gentle (about 20 days), Standard (about 10 days, recommended for most people) or Responsive (about 5 days).

Set things up in a few minutes

  1. Bring any history you already have. Lightarc imports Libra, Happy Scale and Shotsy CSV files, and most other apps' CSV files, with a preview before anything is saved. If your smart scale's app saves to Health Connect, Lightarc can read your weigh-ins from there (weight only).
  2. Choose your units: pounds, kilograms, or stones and pounds.
  3. Log today's weight. Tap +, set your weight, save.
  4. Add a goal if you want one. It's optional, and it shows as a dashed line on your chart with the distance to go.
  5. Turn on a reminder if it helps you keep the rhythm.

What to look at each week

  • The trend line's direction over the last few weeks.
  • The weekly rate: how fast your trend is moving, worked out from the last 14 days.
  • Change since start. You can set the start date, for example to January 1.

What a trend can't tell you

A trend line describes your own log. It isn't medical advice and doesn't explain why your weight changed. For questions about your health, talk to your doctor.
